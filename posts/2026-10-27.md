I recently tried a proxy client called [Clash Verge Rev](https://github.com/clash-verge-rev/clash-verge-rev) on Linux. It’s often described as one of the most popular Clash clients, and I’d been told the same thing when I asked ChatGPT and Claude for recommendations.

Before version 2.5.8, everything worked fine. After I installed 2.5.8 from the RPM package, which is the installer format used by Fedora and Red Hat, things started falling apart. My connection kept cutting out in short bursts, and the problems got worse over time. At one point I couldn’t even watch a simple YouTube video in 1080p without it stuttering and buffering constantly.

That was my limit. I wanted something that actually works well on Linux.

### Finding a Better Option

While searching, I came across [clash-for-linux](https://github.com/wnlen/clash-for-linux). Unlike Clash Verge Rev, it’s a TUI (terminal user interface), meaning it runs in a text-based terminal window instead of a graphical app with buttons and menus. That might sound intimidating, but setup was just a few simple commands. Once it was running, I could set it to start automatically with my computer and mostly forget about it.

The difference was immediate. The GUI version used more system resources, responded slowly, and kept glitching. The terminal version ran smoothly, responded quickly, and stayed out of my way for the rest of the day.

If you’re having similar problems with a graphical proxy client on Linux, it may be worth trying a lightweight terminal option. Just be sure to read the setup instructions carefully, since they’re written for people comfortable with the command line.
