A Beginner’s Guide to Email Aliases: Protect Your Privacy Online

Published: Oct 30, 2026

Why This Matters: A Recent Example

In October 2026, the game company Capcom confirmed a data breach affecting its Capcom ID service. According to the company, 11,774,247 email addresses were leaked along with account details such as creation dates, login dates, and the IP address used at the last login. Most accounts didn’t have passwords stolen, but the leaked email addresses are still a problem. Capcom warned users to watch out for suspicious emails, since attackers can use leaked addresses to send convincing phishing messages that pretend to come from the company. source

This shows a common risk. If you use one email address for everything, such as games, shopping, newsletters, and banking, then one leak connects all of those accounts to you. Anyone who gets that address can target you across all of them.

An email alias is a simple way to reduce this risk.

What Is an Email Alias?

An email alias is a second email address that forwards messages to your real inbox. You can make a different alias for each website or service. If one alias gets leaked or starts receiving spam, you can turn it off or delete it, and your main address stays private.

Think of it like giving out a different phone number for each store you shop at. Your real number stays hidden, and you can stop a single number without changing your whole phone line.

Option 1: Apple Hide My Email (for iPhone and Mac users)

If you use an iPhone or iCloud account, this is often the easiest option.

  1. Open Settings on your iPhone.
  2. Tap your name at the top.
  3. Tap iCloud.
  4. Tap Hide My Email.
  5. Tap Create New Address, give it a label (like “Shopping”), and tap Continue.
  6. Use the new address when signing up for a website or app.

Messages sent to that address will arrive in your normal inbox.

Option 2: Firefox Relay (works on any device)

Firefox Relay is a free service from Mozilla, the company behind Firefox.

  1. Go to relay.firefox.com and sign in with a Firefox account (free to create).
  2. Click Create a mask (Mozilla’s name for an alias).
  3. Copy the new address.
  4. Use it when signing up for a service. Emails sent to it will be forwarded to your real inbox.

You can disable or delete any mask at any time from the same page.

Tips for Using Aliases Well

  • Use a different alias for each service. Create one for games, one for shopping, and one for newsletters.
  • Turn off aliases that get spam. If an alias receives unwanted mail, disable it. This is one of the main benefits of aliases.
  • Keep your real address for important accounts. Use your main email for banking, taxes, and government services, where you need a reliable address.
  • Still be careful with emails. Even with aliases, check the sender before clicking links, and go directly to a website instead of using links in unexpected emails.
  • Save your aliases in a password manager. This helps you remember which alias went with which account.

Quick Checklist

  1. Pick a method: Apple Hide My Email, Firefox Relay, or the Gmail “+” trick.
  2. Create an alias for your next sign-up.
  3. Give each service its own alias when possible.
  4. Disable any alias that starts getting spam.
  5. Stay alert for phishing emails, especially after a known data breach.

Using email aliases takes only a few minutes to set up, and it can limit the damage when a company like Capcom has a breach. Your real email address stays out of the spotlight, and you stay in control of who can reach you.
